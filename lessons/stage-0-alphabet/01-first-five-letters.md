# Lesson 1 — Your First Five Letters

**Stage 0 · Alphabet** · ⏱ 30–45 min · New letters: **А К М О Т**

## 🎯 Goals

By the end of this lesson you can:

- recognise and say five Cyrillic letters (capital and small);
- read your first ten Russian words and a few mini-sentences;
- use **stress marks** (´) and understand that Russian has no "a/the" and no "is".

---

## 1. Three facts before we start

1. **Cyrillic is easy.** Only 33 letters, and Russian spelling is far more regular than English. Most beginners can read the whole alphabet within a week.
2. **You already know some of it.** Five letters look *and* sound almost exactly like English. Those are today's.
3. **Stress matters.** Every Russian word of two or more syllables has one stressed syllable, and it is *not* shown in normal Russian writing. In this course we mark it with an acute accent (´) — **ма́ма** — and in the respelling we write it in CAPITALS: *MA-ma*. One-syllable words never need a mark.

> **How to read the respellings in this course:** they show how a word *sounds* to an English speaker (CAPS = stressed syllable). They are training wheels — we drop them after Stage 0.

---

## 2. The five "true friends"

| Letter | Sound | Think of… | Notes |
|:---:|:---:|---|---|
| **А а** | *ah* | **a** in *f**a**ther* | Open mouth, relaxed. Never "ay" as in *cake*. |
| **К к** | *k* | **k** in *s**k**y* | Say it with **no puff of air** (hold your hand in front of your mouth: it shouldn't move much). |
| **М м** | *m* | **m** in *mom* | Same as English. |
| **О о** | *oh* | **o** in *m**o**re*, but shorter | Pure vowel — keep your lips rounded and don't glide into "ow". |
| **Т т** | *t* | **t** in *s**t**op* | Touch the **tip of your tongue to the back of your upper teeth**, not the ridge behind them. No puff of air. |

Small and capital forms are the same shapes in different sizes (**К к, М м, Т т**; **А а, О о**). In print, small **т** looks like a small capital T — that's normal.

> 🔊 **Do this now:** say each sound aloud five times. Then record yourself and compare with a native speaker at [forvo.com](https://forvo.com) (search the word, pick a Russian speaker).

## 3. Warm-up: syllables

Read these aloud, slowly, then faster. Each syllable is one consonant + one vowel (or the reverse).

| | | | | | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ма | мо | ка | ко | та | то |
| ам | ом | ак | ок | ат | от |

Now blend two syllables: **ма-ма**, **та-та**, **ка-та**, **то-ка**, **мо-ка**. (Some are not real words — that's fine, this is just for your mouth.)

## 4. Your first words

| Russian | Respelling | English |
|---|---|---|
| **а** | *ah* | and / but (a tiny, very common word) |
| **ма́ма** | *MA-ma* | mom |
| **там** | *tam* | there |
| **так** | *tak* | so; like this; that way |
| **как** | *kak* | how; as |
| **кто** | *kto* | who |
| **кот** | *kot* | cat (a male cat — or "cat" in general) |
| **мак** | *mak* | poppy |
| **том** | *tom* | volume (a book in a series) |
| **акт** | *akt* | act (of a play); official document |

> ⚠️ In **ма́ма**, the stress is on the **first** syllable. The second **а** is weaker and shorter: *MA-muh*. Russian vowels get lazy when they are not stressed — more on that in Lesson 7.

**Tip:** in **кто**, the **к** and **т** are pronounced together without a vowel between them: *k-to*, not *ka-to*.

---

## 5. A peek at grammar (it's really simple today)

Russian has **no articles** (*a, an, the*) and, in the present tense, **no word for "is/am/are"**. So you can build a full sentence from just a place word and a noun:

| Russian | Literally | Natural English |
|---|---|---|
| **Там ма́ма.** | There mom. | Mom is there. |
| **Там кот.** | There cat. | The cat is there. / There's a cat there. |
| **Кто там?** | Who there? | Who's there? |
| **Как там?** | How there? | How is it there? |

Notice: capital letter at the start, full stop or question mark at the end — just like English. (Russian question marks look the same as ours.)

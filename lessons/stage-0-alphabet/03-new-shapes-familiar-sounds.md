## 2. The eight letters

These don't look like anything in English (or look like something else entirely) — but you already know how they sound.

| Letter | Sound | English cue | Memory hook |
|:---:|:---:|---|---|
| **Б б** | *b* | **b** in *book* | Small **б** looks like a **6** with a flag. **Б**рат = **b**rother. |
| **Г г** | *g* | **g** in *go* | Looks like a gallows or a backwards "L". **Г**де? |
| **Д д** | *d* | **d** in *dog* | Capital **Д** is a little **house on legs** → **Д**ом (house). Tongue touches the teeth. |
| **З з** | *z* | **z** in *zoo* | **З** is a number **3** — "3" = "z". |
| **И и** | *ee* | **ee** in *see* | A **backwards N**. And the word **и** means "and". |
| **Л л** | *l* | **l** in *milk* / *full* ("dark l") | Looks like a roof Λ (lambda). Put the **tongue tip against your teeth**. |
| **П п** | *p* | **p** in *spin* (no puff of air) | Like the Greek **π**. **П**а́па = dad. |
| **Ф ф** | *f* | **f** in *fun* | A circle on a stick. **Ф**утбо́л. |

### Two things to keep in mind

**1. Letters pronounced harder than they look.** Russian **т, д, н, л** (and **с, з**) are made with the tongue on the **back of the upper teeth**. This makes them sound crisper than English. Practise: *та-да-на-ла*.

**2. Spelling ≠ sound at the end of a word.** Russian "devoices" voiced consonants at the end of a word:

| Spelled | Sounds like |
|:---:|:---:|
| **б** | *p* |
| **в** | *f* |
| **г** | *k* |
| **д** | *t* |
| **з** | *s* |

So **друг** (friend) sounds like *drook*, **сад** (garden) sounds like *sat*, **хлеб** (bread) like *khlyep*. This is automatic once you hear it — the "Respelling" column below already shows the real sound. Full details in Lesson 7.

## 5. Grammar peek — questions and "where"

**Yes/no questions** need no special word or word order: you just raise your voice on the key word.

| Statement | Question |
|---|---|
| **Там ма́ма.** — Mom is there. | **Там ма́ма?** — Is mom there? |
| **Там такси́.** — A taxi is there. | **Там такси́?** — Is there a taxi there? |

Answer with **да** or **нет**:

> — **Там па́па?** — Is dad there?
> — **Нет, там брат.** — No, my brother's there.

**Where-questions** use **где**; the answer is usually **там / тут / вот**:

> — **Где метро́?** — Where's the metro?
> — **Вот метро́.** / **Там.** — There it is. / There.

### Mini-dialogues — read aloud, then translate

**A**
> — Приве́т, Анто́н!
> — Приве́т, Ле́на! Где Бори́с?
> — Вот он.
> — Спаси́бо! Пока́!

**B**
> — Извини́те, где туале́т?
> — Там.
> — Спаси́бо.

# Lesson 4 — Vowels, Hard & Soft

**Stage 0 · Alphabet** · ⏱ 50–60 min · New letters: **Э Ы Я Ё Ю Й**
Already known: А К М О Т В Н Р С У Х Е Б Г Д З И Л П Ф (20 letters) → after today **26 of 33**.

## 🎯 Goals

- Understand the Russian vowel system: **5 hard vowels + 5 soft vowels**;
- learn **Э Ы Я Ё Ю Й**;
- learn all the personal pronouns (**я, ты, он, она́, мы, вы, они́**), **э́то** ("this is") and **мой / моя́ / моё / мои́** ("my");
- read countries and names, and say *"I love…"*.

## 2. The key idea: ten vowel letters in five pairs

Russian has only **5 vowel sounds** but **10 vowel letters**. Each sound has a **hard** letter and a **soft** twin:

| | **А** | **Э** | **Ы** | **О** | **У** |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **Hard** | а | э | ы | о | у |
| **Soft** | **Я** | **Е** | **И** | **Ё** | **Ю** |
| **Sounds like** | *ya* | *ye* | *ee* | *yo* | *yu* |

What does "soft" actually *do*?

1. **At the start of a word or after another vowel,** the soft vowels add a **"y"** in front: **я** = *ya*, **е** = *ye*, **ё** = *yo*, **ю** = *yu*. (**И** has no extra "y" — it is simply *ee*.)
2. **After a consonant,** the soft vowel makes **the consonant soft** too: the middle of your tongue lifts toward the roof of the mouth. Compare English *"nu"* in *new* (British: *nyoo*) with *"noo"* in *noon*. Russian **ню** vs **ну** is exactly that difference. This "palatalisation" is what gives Russian its character, and we'll practise it a lot.

| Hard syllable | Soft syllable |
|:---:|:---:|
| на *na* | ня *nya* (like "ca**ny**on") |
| ту *too* | тю *tyoo* |
| мы *mih* | ми *mee* |
| ло *lo* | лё *lyo* |

You already met **Е** (*ye*) and **И** (*ee*); today you meet the other three soft vowels and the three hard ones you haven't seen.

## 3. The six new letters

### Hard vowels

| Letter | Sound | Notes |
|:---:|:---:|---|
| **Э э** | *e* as in *b**e**d* | Looks like a **backwards Е**. Appears mostly at the start of words: **э́то**, **э́хо**, **экра́н**. |
| **Ы ы** | *ih* — the tricky one | No English equivalent. Say **ee**, then pull your tongue **back and down** (like the *i* in *b**i**ll*, but further back). It looks like **ь + I**: "**ы**". Never starts a word. |

> 🔎 **Ы vs И:** **мы** (*mih*, "we") and **ми** (*mee*, the note *mi*) differ only in this vowel. Hard **ы** goes after hard consonants; soft **и** after soft ones.

### Soft vowels

| Letter | Sound | Notes |
|:---:|:---:|---|
| **Я я** | *ya* as in *yard* | A **backwards R**. The word **я** means "I" and — unlike English — is **not** capitalised in the middle of a sentence. |
| **Ё ё** | *yo* as in *yonder* | **Always stressed** — so never add ´ to it. ⚠️ In everyday Russian the two dots are usually *left off* (people write **е**), but in this course we always write them. |
| **Ю ю** | *yu* as in *you* | Looks like **I + O**. |

### The semi-vowel

| Letter | Sound | Notes |
|:---:|:---:|---|
| **Й й** | *y* as in *boy* | "**и кра́ткое**" = *short i* — an **и** with a little hat. It's a **consonant** that glides after a vowel: **ай** ≈ *eye*, **ой** ≈ *oy* (boy), **ей** ≈ *ay* (day), **уй** ≈ *ooy*, **ый** ≈ *ih-y*, **ий** ≈ *ee-y*. It never makes its own syllable, so **мой** has **one** vowel and **one** syllable. |

## 4. Words

### Pronouns — *the most important table of the lesson*

| Russian | Respelling | English |
|---|---|---|
| **я** | *ya* | I |
| **ты** | *tih* | you (informal, one person) |
| **он** | *on* | he |
| **она́** | *a-NA* | she |
| **оно́** | *a-NO* | it (neuter) |
| **мы** | *mih* | we |
| **вы** | *vih* | you (formal, **or** more than one person) |
| **они́** | *a-NEE* | they |

> **ты vs вы:** use **ты** for friends, family, children and peers. Use **вы** for one person you don't know well, anyone older or in a formal setting — **and** for any group of people. Using **ты** with a stranger can sound rude; using **вы** with a friend sounds cold. When in doubt, use **вы**.

### Useful little words

| Russian | Respelling | English |
|---|---|---|
| **э́то** | *E-ta* | this is; it's; these are |
| **не** | *nye* | not |
| **люблю́** | *lyub-LYU* | I love; I like |

### "My" — it changes with the noun's gender

| Russian | Respelling | English |
|---|---|---|
| **мой** | *moy* | my (masculine noun: **мой дом**) |
| **моя́** | *ma-YA* | my (feminine noun: **моя́ сестра́**) |
| **моё** | *ma-YO* | my (neuter noun: **моё молоко́**) |
| **мои́** | *ma-EE* | my (plural: **мои́ биле́ты**) |
| **твой** | *tvoy* | your, informal (m.) |
| **твоя́** | *tva-YA* | your, informal (f.) |
| **твоё** | *tva-YO* | your, informal (n.) |
| **твои́** | *tva-EE* | your, informal (pl.) |

Russian nouns have **three genders**. A quick rule you'll use constantly: nouns ending in a **consonant** are usually **masculine** (**дом, друг, брат**), nouns ending in **-а / -я** are usually **feminine** (**сестра́, ма́ма**), and nouns ending in **-о / -е / -ё** are usually **neuter** (**молоко́, мо́ре**). Today you only need to *notice* it.

## 5. Grammar peek

### a) "This is…" — **Э́то** + noun
**Э́то** works for "this is / that is / it's / these are" — for any gender, singular or plural, and again **no verb**:

| Russian | English |
|---|---|
| **Э́то мой дом.** | This is my house. |
| **Э́то моя́ сестра́.** | This is my sister. |
| **Э́то моё молоко́.** | This is my milk. |
| **Э́то мои́ биле́ты.** | These are my tickets. *(plural: **биле́т → биле́ты**)* |
| **Кто э́то?** — **Э́то Та́ня.** | Who is this? — This is Tanya. |
| **Я Анто́н.** | I'm Anton. *(no "am"!)* |

### b) Saying "not" — **не**
**Э́то не рестора́н, а теа́тр.** — This is not a restaurant, but a theatre. *(**а** = "but" in contrast, as you learned in Lesson 1.)*

### c) "I love…"
**Я люблю́ ко́фе.** · **Я люблю́ футбо́л.** · **Я люблю́ теа́тр.**
*(We'll learn how the noun changes for other words in Stage 1 — these nouns don't change.)*

### d) A teaser — "in"
**Я в Москве́.** — I'm in Moscow. Notice **Москва́ → Москве́** after **в**: Russian nouns change their endings depending on their job in the sentence. That's the famous case system — Stage 1 starts it gently. Today, just notice it.

### Mini-dialogue

> — Кто э́то?
> — Э́то мой друг Анто́н.
> — Приве́т, Анто́н! Я Ле́на.
> — Приве́т, Ле́на!

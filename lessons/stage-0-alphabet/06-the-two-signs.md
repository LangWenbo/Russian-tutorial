## 2. The soft sign **Ь** (мя́гкий знак)

**Ь has no sound.** It only tells you that the consonant before it is **soft** (palatalised). In our respellings, an apostrophe (**'**) means "soft".

### Hard vs soft consonants

Almost every Russian consonant comes in a **hard/soft pair**:

| hard | б | в | г | д | з | к | л | м | н | п | р | с | т | ф | х |
|:---|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| **soft** | бь | вь | гь | дь | зь | кь | ль | мь | нь | пь | рь | сь | ть | фь | хь |

*Exceptions:* **ж, ш, ц** are always hard; **ч, щ, й** are always soft.

How to make a soft consonant: say the consonant while **raising the middle of your tongue toward the roof of your mouth**, as if you were about to say "y" or "ee".

| Soft | Like in English… |
|:---:|---|
| **нь** | *ca**ny**on* — the "ny" |
| **ль** | *mi**ll**ion* — the "ly" |
| **ть** | British *t**u**ne* — "ty" |

The same softening happens **before soft vowels (я е ё ю и)** — you've been doing it since Lesson 4 (**ня, нё, ни**). **Ь** does the same job when there's *no* vowel after: at the end of a word (**день**) or before another consonant (**ма́льчик**).

### Hard vs soft changes meaning!

| Hard | | Soft | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **мат** *mat* | checkmate | **мать** *mat'* | mother |
| **брат** *brat* | brother | **брать** *brat'* | to take |
| **лук** *look* | onion | **люк** *lyook* | hatch, manhole |
| **нос** *nos* | nose | **нёс** *nyos* | (he) carried |
| **у́гол** *OO-gal* | corner | **у́голь** *OO-gal'* | coal |

> 🗣 **Practice:** say *"ma-t, ma-t'"*, *"bra-t, bra-t'"* — hear the tiny "y" tail at the end of the soft one.

### Three jobs of Ь

1. **Softens the consonant before it** → **день** *dyen'*, **пять** *pyat'*, **соль** *sol'*, **ма́льчик** *MAL'-cheek*.
2. **Separates** a consonant from a following **я, е, ё, ю, и**, so that you hear the **"y"**: **семья́** = *seem-YA* ("sem" + "ya"), **друзья́** *drooz-YA*, **пла́тье** *PLAT-ye*.
3. **Grammar marker:** it appears at the end of many feminine nouns (**дверь, ночь**) and of nearly all **infinitives** (**чита́ть**). After **ж, ш, ч, щ** it doesn't change the sound at all — it's pure grammar (**ночь**, **дочь**).

## 3. The hard sign **Ъ** (твёрдый знак)

**Ъ** is rare and has no sound. It sits **between a prefix ending in a consonant and a root starting with я, е, ё, ю** — it says *"pronounce the **y**, keep the consonant hard"*. It never starts a word.

| Without **Ъ** | | With **Ъ** | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **сесть** *syest'* | to sit down | **съесть** *s-YEST'* | to eat up |

More examples: **объе́кт** *ab-YEKT* (object) · **подъе́зд** *pad-YEZD* (entrance/stairwell of an apartment building) · **объясни́ть** *ab-yas-NEET'* (to explain)

## 5. Grammar peek

### a) The dash = "is"
When both sides of a sentence are nouns, Russian often writes a **dash** where English has "is/are":

> **Анто́н — учи́тель.** Anton is a teacher.
> **Мой брат — врач.** My brother is a doctor.
> **Я студе́нт.** / **Я студе́нтка.** I'm a student. *(with **я**, no dash needed)*

### b) Telling people what you do
> **Я врач.** · **Я учи́тель.** (f.: **Я учи́тельница.**) · **Я студе́нтка.** · **Я журнали́ст.** · **Я актри́са.**
> **Я не врач, а учи́тель.** I'm not a doctor but a teacher.

### Reading passage — read aloud, then answer the questions in Exercise 5

> Меня́ зову́т Ната́ша. Я врач. Э́то мой муж Бори́с. Он учи́тель. Э́то моя́ дочь Ма́ша. Она́ студе́нтка. Мы в Москве́. Э́то моя́ семья́!

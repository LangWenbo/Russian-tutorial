## 2. Stress

* Every Russian word of two or more syllables has **exactly one** stressed syllable.
* It can fall on **any** syllable and it often **moves** when the word changes form (**рука́ → ру́ки**, "hand → hands"). So there is no rule to guess it. **Always learn a word together with its stress.** That's why this course marks every stress (´) — and why you'll never find a vocabulary list in this course without them.
* **Ё** is *always* stressed (so we don't mark it).
* A stressed vowel is **longer, louder and clearer**; an unstressed vowel is **short, weak and changes colour** (see below).
* Stress can change the meaning:

| Stress 1 | Stress 2 |
|---|---|
| **за́мок** *ZA-mak* — castle | **замо́к** *za-MOK* — lock |
| **му́ка** *MOO-ka* — torment | **мука́** *moo-KA* — flour |

* Small words (**в, на, к, с, у, не, и, а, но**) have no stress of their own; they "lean on" the next word and are said as **one unit**: **в шко́ле** → *fSHKO-lye*; **не зна́ю** → *nye-ZNA-yu*.

## 3. Vowel reduction — *the most important rule*

Because unstressed vowels get weaker, **what you see is not exactly what you say.**

### О → "а" (unstressed)

An unstressed **о** sounds like **а** (very short):

| Spelling | Sounds like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **молоко́** | *ma-la-KO* | milk |
| **хорошо́** | *kha-ra-SHO* | good |
| **голова́** | *ga-la-VA* | head |
| **соба́ка** | *sa-BA-ka* | dog |
| **доро́га** | *da-RO-ga* | road |
| **спаси́бо** | *spa-SEE-ba* | thanks |
| **Москва́** | *mask-VA* | Moscow |

Two small levels: the syllable **right before** the stress is a short **a** (*ha-ra-SHO*); the others are very weak, almost *uh* (*ma-la-KO* — the middle one is a murmur). Just say a short **а** and you'll be understood.

### Е, Я → "и" (unstressed)

Unstressed **е** and **я** (inside a word) sound like a short **ee** / **i**:

| Spelling | Sounds like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **сестра́** | *sees-TRA* | sister |
| **язы́к** | *yee-ZIHK* | language |
| **метро́** | *mee-TRO* | metro |
| **челове́к** | *chee-la-VYEK* | person (**е** → *ee*, **о** → *a*, stressed **е** = *ye*) |
| **телефо́н** | *tee-lee-FON* | telephone |

### Vowels that don't change
Unstressed **у, ы, и, э** stay essentially the same (just shorter). And the vowel **а** unstressed is also weak (*MA-muh*).

> 🧠 **Rule of thumb:** stressed = clear & long; unstressed = short & lazy; **о/а** blend into a short *a*; **е/я/и** blend into a short *i*.

## 4. Consonant rules

### a) Final devoicing

Russian has **voiced/voiceless pairs**:

| voiced | б | в | г | д | ж | з |
|:---|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| **voiceless** | п | ф | к | т | ш | с |

At the **end of a word**, voiced consonants are pronounced **voiceless**:

| Spelling | Sounds like |
|---|---|
| **хлеб** | *khlyep* |
| **друг** | *drook* |
| **сад** | *sat* |
| **нож** | *nosh* |
| **глаз** (eye) | *glas* |
| **Оле́г** | *a-LYEK* |

### b) Assimilation (a consonant takes on the voice of its neighbour)

Look at the **next** sound:

* **Voiced before voiceless → voiceless:**
  **вход** (entrance) → *fkhot* · **ло́жка** (spoon) → *LOSH-ka* · **кни́жка** (little book) → *KNEESH-ka* · **авто́бус** (bus) → *af-TO-boos* · **в па́рке** → *f PAR-kye*
* **Voiceless before voiced → voiced:**
  **вокза́л** (train station) → *vag-ZAL* · **футбо́л** → *food-BOL* · **сде́лать** (to do) → *ZDYE-lat'* · **экза́мен** (exam) → *eeg-ZA-meen*

> The letters **м, н, л, р, й** don't change their neighbours; **х, ц, ч, щ** are always voiceless.

### c) Silent letters (in "difficult clusters")

| Spelling | Sounds like | Meaning | Silent letter |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Здра́вствуйте** | *ZDRAST-vooy-tye* | hello (formal) | first **в** |
| **со́лнце** | *SON-tse* | sun | **л** |
| **се́рдце** | *SYER-tse* | heart | **д** |
| **пра́здник** | *PRAZ-neek* | holiday | **д** |
| **и́звестный** | *eez-VYES-nihy* | famous | **т** |
| **чу́вство** | *CHOOST-va* | feeling | **в** |
| **ле́стница** | *LYES-nee-tsa* | staircase | **т** |

### d) Special spellings you simply must know

| Spelling | Sounds like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **что** | *shto* | what |
| **что́бы** | *SHTO-bih* | in order to |
| **коне́чно** | *ka-NYESH-na* | of course |
| **ску́чно** | *SKOOSH-na* | boring |
| **его́** | *yee-VO* | his; him (**г** = *v*) |
| **ничего́** | *nee-chee-VO* | nothing; it's OK |
| **сего́дня** | *see-VOD-nya* | today |
| **учи́ться** | *oo-CHEE-tsa* | to study (**-ться** = *tsa*) |
| **нра́виться** | *NRA-vee-tsa* | to like (be pleasing) |

> The ending **-ого / -его** (in adjectives and pronouns) is pronounced **-ово / -ево**. You'll meet it again and again.

### e) **Ж, Ш, Ц** + **и**/**е**
After **ж, ш, ц** (always hard), the letter **и** sounds like **ы** (**жить** *zhiht'*, **маши́на** *ma-SHIH-na*, **цирк** *tsirk*) and an unstressed **е** sounds like **ы** (**жена́** *zhih-NA*).

## 5. Intonation

Russian intonation is simpler than English once you see the pattern.

* **Statements:** the voice **falls** on the last important word. **Э́то мой дом.** ↘
* **Questions with a question word** (**кто, что, где, как…**): same falling pattern. **Где метро́?** ↘ **Как дела́?** ↘
* **Yes/no questions:** there's a **sharp rise on the stressed syllable of the word you're asking about**, then the voice **drops** *(unlike English, which rises at the end)*:
  * **Э́то твой ДОМ?** — *Is it your HOUSE?* (or something else?)
  * **Э́то ТВОЙ дом?** — *Is it YOUR house?* (or somebody else's?)
  * **ЭТО твой дом?** — *Is THIS your house?* (or that one?)

> 🎧 *Listen to native speakers — the intonation can't be learned from text alone. Search for a phrase on [forvo.com](https://forvo.com) or in a video, and imitate.*

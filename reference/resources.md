# Resources

Your tutor and the lessons are the core of this course. These tools help you practise everything between lessons. *(Availability and prices change — check each one.)*

## Dictionaries & pronunciation
- **[Wiktionary (English)](https://en.wiktionary.org)** — shows **stress marks**, declension/conjugation tables, example sentences and often audio. Your first stop when unsure about a word.
- **[Forvo](https://forvo.com)** — recordings of words by native speakers. Search a word, pick a Russian speaker.
- **[Reverso Context](https://context.reverso.net)** — real-life example sentences for words and phrases.
- **Yandex Translate** / **Google Translate** — handy for listening to text-to-speech; don't trust them blindly for grammar.

## Flashcards
- **[Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net)** — spaced repetition. This repo exports flashcards per lesson — see [`../vocabulary/README.md`](../vocabulary/README.md).

## Listening & watching (start at A2; earlier if you enjoy it)
- **Easy Russian** (YouTube) — street interviews with Russian and English subtitles; very good for real speech.
- **Real Russian Club** (YouTube) — teaching videos by Russian teachers.
- **Russian cartoons** (e.g. *Союзмультфильм*, *Маша и Медведь*) — slow, clear language and a lot of repetition; excellent from A2.
- **Music** — read the lyrics while you listen; it trains rhythm and stress.
- Podcasts for learners — search "Russian podcast for learners" for your level.

## Reading
- **Graded readers** for A1–B2 (search *"Russian graded reader"* or *"Russian adapted text"*).
- **Short stories** — Chekhov (B2+ at first with notes), Pushkin's fairy tales, children's stories (*Чуковский*).
- **Russian Wikipedia** (ru.wikipedia.org) — start with topics you already know.
- Signs, menus, supermarket labels — read everything.

## Textbooks (optional supplements)
- *The New Penguin Russian Course* — Nicholas J. Brown (self-study, grammar-heavy)
- *Colloquial Russian* — Svetlana le Fleming & Susan Kay
- *Golosa* — Richard Robin et al. (university course; has audio)
- *A Comprehensive Russian Grammar* — Terence Wade (reference grammar)
- A dedicated **verbs-of-motion workbook** (search "Russian verbs of motion exercises") — the area where extra practice pays off most at A2–B1.

## Conversation
- Online tutors (e.g. on italki, Preply) — start once you've done Stage 1 and want to speak with a human.
- Language-exchange apps and communities (Tandem, HelloTalk, r/russian) — remember your tutor in this repo is also a speaking partner: type to it in Russian every day.

## Exams
- **ТРКИ / TORFL** — the official Russian proficiency exam, with levels from A1 (ТЭУ) to C2 (ТРКИ-4). Sample papers are published by Russian universities (e.g. Moscow State University, RUDN, SPbU) and the exam centres.
